# Roadside rhythm, motivation and review

The reviewed design already distinguishes Sweetser/Wyeth GameFlow from Valve's
AI Director. Sources were rechecked2026-10-25. Valve's [Michael Booth2009 slides](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
separate build-up/peak/fade/relax pacing. Transferring those ideas to racing means
varying required decisions and hazard encounters while retaining speed cues. It
does not imply that every quiet stretch must be empty or that danger should be
randomly intensified after a mistake.

A complementary motivation lens is [Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski2006](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf).
Their studies associate perceived competence/autonomy with enjoyment and future
play. This is not a racing-camera experiment. Cannonball inferences: clear,
forgiving contact feedback supports learning; route choices preserve autonomy;
distinctive roadside businesses reward noticing the world without requiring a
new decision at every object. Neither study validates a specific spacing rule.

## Current bounded experiment

Four original pictorial businesses (gas, burger, motel, diner) appear at six
fixed authored stops, alternating sides and styles. They replace no road hazard,
do not grant rewards, and cannot appear across ramp pavement. Existing repeated
foliage is a speed reference; rare businesses provide memorable landmarks between
those rhythms. This is a small art sample, not a verified nationwide region kit.

The content director should eventually budget both visual beats and decisions:
1. Quiet stretches retain bridge rails/posts/road texture as flow references.
2. One distinctive business or landmark can punctuate a quieter approach.
3. Keep lane-choice/construction warnings visually separate from businesses.
4. Avoid presenting a unique art beat, complex junction and radio instruction at
   the same instant. Recovery reduces choices, not apparent forward speed.
5. Review repeated routes for recognition and boredom before generating more art.

Observe whether players see a warning before acting, distinguish near-miss from
impact, recognize a roadside stop on retry and can steer without fighting assist.
Count unjustified crashes and missed cues; subjective speed/comfort ratings and
actual phone play remain essential. A source citation does not replace those
checks. Camera and station-surf timings remain reviewable authored values.
